AMBER — the phosphor terminal

A warm P3-amber tube in a real bezel: the command already typed, the block cursor waiting. Nostalgic, confident, adult — depth from glass, grille, scanlines and bloom, not from a flat dark card.

Why it works

  • P3 amber on a curved, vignetted tube: warmth and depth a flat dark card cannot fake.
  • The command is already typed — chevron plus blinking block cursor make the README reader the operator.
  • Aperture grille, scanlines, glass glare, bezel hardware: texture earned, not applied. A faint dig answer hides in the phosphor.

XY — the vectorscope

The wordmark drawn the way an electron beam draws it: single-stroke letterforms on a P31 storage scope, with graticule, Lissajous underlay and a front panel of knobs. Lab gear for people who read packet captures — and the bottom trace tells the product story: one spike, filtered out.

Why it works

  • The wordmark is an electron beam: single-stroke letterforms, dwell-bright joints, phosphor afterglow.
  • The bottom trace is the pitch — a pulse train with one spike clamped flat. Filtering, told in instrument language.
  • Graticule, Lissajous underlay, knobs and BNC: it reads as a bench instrument, not a dev-tool template.

FANFOLD — the printout

A 132-column line-printer answer section: overstruck Courier, tractor-feed holes, a column ruler, and a red NOERROR stamp where a signature would go. DNS as paperwork — light by default, tactile, and funny exactly the way dig output is funny.

Why it works

  • A real artifact, not a theme: overstruck Courier, ink-rough edges (displacement, not a texture PNG), tractor holes, column ruler.
  • Light by default — it stands out in a feed of near-black dev-tool banners, and still carries its own background on dark READMEs.
  • The jokes are resolver jokes: the tagline ships as a TXT record, and NOERROR is the stamp of a query gone right.
